LatiFi never holds your private keys or your funds. This is an academic demonstration on a test network, and the money in the app has no real value.

1. Acceptance

By opening this website or using the LatiFi Wallet app, you accept these terms. If you do not accept them, please do not use LatiFi.

LatiFi is a student project for the course SI728 Emerging Software Architectures at UPC. It is not a bank and it does not lend money itself. A team of students from the course builds it.

2. What LatiFi is

LatiFi connects people who need a small loan with people who want to lend. You can borrow or lend, and each loan is agreed between the two wallets involved.

The project has three parts. The LatiFi Wallet app for Android is where you create your wallet, ask for a loan, fund a request and pay installments. The LatiFi API keeps profiles, calculates reputation and provides exchange rates. Smart contracts on the Polygon Amoy test network carry out the loans.

This website explains the project and publishes these documents. The app works on Android only.

3. Test network and test token

Everything in LatiFi runs on Polygon Amoy, a public test network. The money in the app is a test token created for this project. It has no real value, and you cannot exchange it for real money.

Anyone can create test tokens for their own wallet, up to a fixed limit. Network fees use a second test token. The team can send you some for the demonstration.

The app also shows amounts in soles and in other local currencies. A third party supplies the exchange rate, and the figures are for reference only. They are not a price, a quote or an offer.

4. Your wallet and your keys

The app creates your wallet on your phone. Your private key stays on your phone, protected by the secure key storage of Android, and it never reaches LatiFi. LatiFi cannot move your money.

When you create your wallet, the app shows you a recovery phrase once. Write it down and keep it offline. Nobody can recover it for you, not even LatiFi. If you lose it, you lose access to your wallet. The app turns off Android backup, so the phrase is the only way to restore your wallet on a new phone.

You sign in by signing a message with your wallet. There is no password. You can also import a wallet you already own.

Every transaction on the blockchain needs a small network fee, and nobody can reverse a transaction after it is confirmed.

5. Loans between users

A borrower publishes a request with an amount, an interest rate, a term of 1 to 365 days and 1 to 6 equal installments. The borrower proposes the rate. The rate is simple annual interest, up to 50 percent a year.

Another user can fund the request. Funding sends the money from the lender's wallet to the borrower's wallet through the contract, in a single transaction. After that, nobody can change the amount, the rate, the term or the installments, and the total to repay is fixed.

The borrower pays each installment from their own wallet to the lender's wallet through the contract. The contract accepts a late payment and records it as late. If an installment stays unpaid for more than 7 days after its due date, anyone can mark the loan as defaulted, and from then on the contract no longer accepts payments.

A request cannot be cancelled after it is published. It stays open until a lender funds it. There is no collateral: nothing is locked as a guarantee, and reputation takes its place.

The loan is between the lender and the borrower. LatiFi does not lend, does not collect payments and does not guarantee that a borrower will repay.

6. Reputation

Every wallet has a reputation, a number from 0 to 1000 with four levels: New, Building, Trusted and Excellent. Lenders see it next to each request.

The number comes from the payments that the contract records (on time, late or defaulted) and from whether your profile is complete. The API recalculates it from those records. It is a signal about your payment history in LatiFi. It is not a credit report.

The score can also go down: each late payment and each default lowers it.

7. Acceptable use

Use LatiFi honestly. Please do not:

  • use someone else's identity, or a display name that misleads other users;
  • create fake loans between wallets you control to raise your reputation;
  • put private information, such as an address, an ID number or a phone number, in your display name, because everyone can read it;
  • try to break, overload or bypass the contract, the API, the app or this website.

The API limits how often you can ask for a sign-in, to protect the service. If you reach the limit, wait a moment and try again.

LatiFi cannot reverse what the contract records, so think before you sign a transaction.

The software can contain mistakes. The test network can restart, change or stop. The API runs on a single server, so it can go offline during a demonstration.

Because the token has no real value, using LatiFi should not cost you real money. Do not use real money or tokens of real value with LatiFi.

You are responsible for your phone, your recovery phrase and the choices you make with other users. As far as the law allows, the team is not liable for losses that result from using LatiFi.
